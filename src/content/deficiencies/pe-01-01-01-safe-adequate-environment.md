## What the Surveyor Is Looking For

Joint Commission surveyors use tracer methodology during the Building Tour. Pest activity, pest evidence (droppings, gnaw marks, harborage), and exclusion failures observed on the tour are documented on the Survey Activity Report and cited to PE.01.01.01 with a narrative description; there is no discrete pest EP to cite. The finding is placed on the SAFER Matrix by likelihood of harm and scope, and a systemic program failure may be cited at the standard level rather than the EP level, with broader scope.

The surveyor's follow-up is the same as under the CMS tags: the written plan, the service records for the area, the threshold that should have triggered action, and who in the hospital knew.

## Documentation That Satisfies It

Carried from the authority page's survey-readiness list for PE.01.01.01:

- A written pest management plan addressing high-risk areas
- Service records demonstrating regular monitoring and intervention
- Trend reports showing pest activity over time
- Evidence of corrective action in response to observed activity

To which a survey-response reader should add the work orders for exclusion repairs and evidence of infection prevention involvement, because both are asked about.

## Where Facilities Most Commonly Fail

Carried from the authority page's list of common findings: pest droppings observed in food preparation areas, evidence of rodent activity in storage rooms, and contracted vendors using uncredentialed applicators where the service agreement specifies credentialed service. Behind each is the same program gap: an area not covered by the plan, a threshold not defined, or a record that shows chemical application and nothing else.

## Evidence of Standards Compliance: What It Must Address

Joint Commission findings are answered with an Evidence of Standards Compliance (ESC) submission rather than a CMS-style plan of correction. The elements an ESC must address for each Requirement for Improvement:

1. **Accountability.** The person or role responsible for the corrective action.
2. **Corrective action.** What was done to bring the specific finding into compliance, with the date it was completed.
3. **Sustained compliance.** How the hospital will ensure the correction holds: the process change, the monitoring, and who reviews it.
4. **Measure of Success, where required.** For findings placed in the higher-risk cells of the SAFER Matrix, a quantifiable measure and the audit that produces it.
